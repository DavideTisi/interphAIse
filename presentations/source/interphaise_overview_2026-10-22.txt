Protective coatings are an important strategy for improving solid-state batteries. Direct contact between a cathode and an electrolyte can cause unwanted reactions and decomposition, producing resistive layers that degrade performance. A coating should suppress these processes while conducting lithium ions and maintaining contact with both materials. [Xiao et al., Joule (2019)](https://ceder.berkeley.edu/publications/Joule2019_coating.pdf) Predicting its behaviour is difficult: crystal orientation, surface termination, lattice strain and atomic disorder can change interface properties, while accurate quantum-mechanical calculations become expensive for large interface models.

interphAIse investigates whether an LLM can take over part of the scientist's intuition: proposing coating candidates, predicting their behaviour and revising choices from computational feedback. Humans define the objective and validation rules; Haiku 5.5 decides what to test next. We use Materials Project structures and energies, pymatgen-based thermodynamic screening at 4 V, and a band-gap filter for electronic insulation. Selected coatings receive atomistic follow-up using the PET-MAD 1.6 r²SCAN machine-learning interatomic potential, ASE and GPU calculations on Niflheim.

Following an initial 50-structure benchmark, we studied 141 candidate structures across three battery environments in 450 runs. In the sulfide environment, iterative selection found an acceptable coating within ten evaluations in 20/30 runs, compared with 2/30 without numerical feedback, 6/30 with shuffled feedback and 8/30 for random search. In LLZO, random search performed better: 24/30 versus 12/30 for the iterative LLM. The chloride task was near ceiling. These controls show that useful feedback can improve decisions, while chemical priors can also mislead.

The PET-MAD follow-up compared LiNbF₆, LiTaF₆ and LiCaAlF₆, giving weaker-contact adhesion values of approximately 1.63, 1.58 and 0.74 J/m². The LLM correctly forecast both LiTaF₆ values but overestimated both LiCaAlF₆ values; its selection order stayed unchanged. The project demonstrates bounded, testable scientific decision-making, not equivalence to human expertise. The interfaces remain formally polar, the Nb–Ta difference is smaller than documented construction sensitivities, and lithium transport and battery cycling remain untested.
